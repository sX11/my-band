# A Sleep & trends page charts seven days of Apple Health; amends ADRs 0001, 0005 and 0006

ADR 0001 cut every chart and the `SleepDetail` hypnogram, and ADRs 0005 and 0006 kept history in Apple Health. The owner wants trends of several readings in the app. So a page left of the Dashboard (`TrendsView`) charts the last seven days: sleep per night by stage, resting heart rate, steps, SpO₂ and weight.

The trends are read from Apple Health each time the page appears, the way Workouts is (ADR 0009). Unlike Workouts, they include every source, because the owner wants the numbers the Health app shows. Steps therefore include the iPhone's, and the page says so. Quantity trends are `HKStatisticsCollectionQuery` daily sums or averages, which merge sources as Health does. Sleep has no such merge, and the iPhone's `asleepUnspecified` overlaps the band's night. So `SleepTrend.nights` counts each minute once and keeps the band's stage where both claim it. A night belongs to the day it ends on, cut at 18:00.

What 0005 and 0006 keep: nothing read here is stored, and the Dashboard stays a status screen. The health data lives on the side pages.

Limits, accepted: the window is fixed at seven days. A locked iPhone refuses the read and the page asks to unlock. If read access to a type is switched off in Health, HealthKit returns nothing for it rather than an error, so that card says there is no data. A nap after 18:00 counts toward the next night.

Rejected: building the trends from SwiftData and the sync snapshot. They hold only the band's data, so steps would not match the Health app, and only sleep is kept day by day. Also rejected: summing sleep samples across sources, which doubles a night the iPhone and the band both recorded. Also dropped: a last-night hypnogram above the trends, from the band's `SleepSession`s. The Health sheet already shows last night's stages (ADR 0006), and the owner wants this page to be only the seven-day charts.

## Addendum (2026-11-12): night sheet, rating and morning summary

Tapping a night's bar, or the latest-night row under the chart, opens a sheet for that night (`NightDetailSheet`). It shows the stages, the main sleep's asleep time and HRV median against the other nights, lowest and average heart rate, average and lowest SpO₂, and a sparkline for each. A strip switches between the window's seven nights. Each night now carries its main sleep: the longest run of asleep minutes in the same per-minute merge the bar uses, with gaps of up to 60 minutes joined. The stage strip, the sheet's asleep time and the vitals cover only that span, so a daytime nap counts in the bar but neither swells the sheet's asleep time nor pulls daytime readings in. Heart rate, HRV and SpO₂ are read as raw samples from every source, one query per type over all seven spans, when the page loads: a median and the sparklines need the points. A failed vitals read fails the page the same way as the other reads.

The sheet also takes a 0–5 rating of how the night felt. This is the first health-like value the app stores, so it amends "nothing read here is stored". The rating is entered here, not read, so it has no other home. It lives in `UserDefaults` (`NightRatings`, keyed by the day the night ends on, pruned after 90 days), not SwiftData, which a corrupt store resets. It is not written to Apple Health: HealthKit has no type for it, and the band's sleep samples are immutable. Deleting the app deletes the ratings.

A morning notification opens the sheet (`MorningSleepNotifier`). A sync that brings in a closed night of at least 3 hours, begun before 03:00 and ending between 03:00 and 12:00 today, posts "Last night: … asleep" with the band's asleep minutes, after 05:00 and once a day; the start rule keeps a long morning nap from reading as last night. It posts only after the night is saved locally and written to Apple Health with its vitals and HRV, so the sheet can read them when tapped. A tap reloads the page and opens the night, or the latest night if Health doesn't have that one yet. The Mi Band 10 sends no wake-up event, so this depends on a sync running in the morning. A fallback covers mornings without one: a fixed-time notification planned 7 mornings ahead, at the first band alarm between 03:00 and 12:00 plus 30 minutes (a one-time alarm only on the morning it rings), or at a picked time. It is replanned when the band's alarm list arrives, after a sync that knows the alarms, and on each foreground, which without the list keeps fallbacks already pending. One replan runs at a time and changes only the requests that differ from the plan. Both share the identifier `morningSleep-<day>`, so a sync's notification replaces that day's fallback, silently if the fallback already showed. Two switches in Band settings turn them off. Following Apple Health's sleep schedule instead was rejected: iOS has no public API for it.
